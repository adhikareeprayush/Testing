My Journey to Organic Farming

I started farming when I was just 18 years old, following in the footsteps of my father and grandfather. Back then, like most farmers in Nepal, I used chemical fertilizers and pesticides without thinking twice. It was what everyone did, and it seemed to work.

The turning point came about 15 years ago when I noticed that the soil on our family farm was becoming harder and less productive each year. We needed more and more chemicals to get the same results. My children were also getting sick more often, and I started to wonder if the chemicals we were using could be the cause.

"When I switched to organic farming, I wasn't just changing how I grow vegetables. I was changing my whole relationship with the land."

That's when I decided to learn about organic farming. I attended workshops organized by local NGOs and connected with other organic farmers in the region. The first few years were challenging – my yields dropped, and some of my neighbors thought I was crazy for giving up "modern" farming methods.

But slowly, things started to change. The soil became softer and richer. Earthworms returned to our fields. The vegetables we grew tasted better than anything I remembered from my childhood. And most importantly, my family's health improved.

Partnering with Organic Shop Nepal

Three years ago, I connected with Organic Shop Nepal, and it changed everything for my farming business. Before, I would sell my vegetables at the local market, often for prices that barely covered my costs. Now, I have a reliable buyer who values quality and pays fair prices for organic produce.

The team at Organic Shop Nepal has helped me improve my farming techniques and even provided training on post-harvest handling to ensure my vegetables reach customers in the best possible condition. They've also helped me get organic certification, which has opened up new opportunities.

Looking to the Future

Today, I farm about 3 hectares of land, growing a variety of organic vegetables including tomatoes, cauliflower, spinach, and seasonal greens. My two sons have also joined me in the farming business – they were initially reluctant, but seeing the success of organic farming has changed their minds. I'm hopeful that organic farming will continue to grow in Nepal, and that more farmers will see the benefits of working with the land rather than against it.
